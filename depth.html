The question behind this experiment

From a faint image
to a human face.

The starting point was a documentary claim: the Shroud contains information from which a three-dimensional face can be reconstructed. Why, then, does simply lifting its bright pixels into space produce such an uneven surface?

A prominent example is HISTORY’s 2010 The Real Face of Jesus?, featuring Ray Downing and Studio Macbeth. Their reconstruction gives this question a concrete reference. The earlier VP-8 work helps explain where the “3D information” idea came from. Read the source trail ↗

What can brightness alone recover? A height map gives each image position one height. Inferring a body beneath draped cloth, then rendering a lifelike face, requires further choices. Here we expose the direct mapping, its processing, and its limits.

What this tests: how much face-like structure appears when image brightness is treated as height. It does not reproduce Downing’s complete workflow or the original VP-8 electronics. An awkward relief from an uncalibrated scan does not by itself refute those studies; a convincing relief does not establish anatomical accuracy or identity.

Read the surface, and its assumptions

What makes a face appear?

Watch what processing changes.

The left view retains the resampled image tones, with the same inversion as the right. Smoothing, contrast stretching, and the tonal exponent affect only the right view. Try the vertical section through the nose and mouth, then switch scans: these are photographic inputs, not calibrated depth measurements.

Start with known depth.

The synthetic control is a schematic face whose actual height values are known. Feeding those heights into the viewer should recover its form. The agreement score compares the displayed height field with that reference.

Change the illumination.

The lit-portrait control uses the same geometry, with side lighting and a darker beard. Its brightness also depends on reflectance and illumination. See which features turn into misleading heights.
