One research agenda / Ten distinct priorities

What would change
our understanding?

Ten studies of the cloth, its image, and its history. Each asks a different question, with a method and a clear standard for interpreting the result.

The aim is to narrow the uncertainties about age, manufacture, image formation, and provenance. Laboratory work, direct examination, and archival research belong in the same agenda. Access requirements are stated for each project.

The reasoning behind the order

Fund questions
that separate explanations.

This is an editorial ranking by likely information gained, usefulness across hypotheses, and feasibility, based on selected primary literature through September 2026. It is not a consensus or an exhaustive review. The first three establish age and material benchmarks; the remaining studies test mechanisms, geometry, manufacture, and history.

Priority is not the order of a laboratory visit. Document the cloth before sampling, collect vulnerable traces before disruptive work, and coordinate illumination with biological analysis. Work needing cloth access or material is conditional on custodial approval. Pilot studies and archives can be pursued in parallel.

The ten studies

One list. A clearer inquiry.

Open a study for its method, decision criteria, and limits.

  1. 01Date linen from across the main cloth.How old is the textile—and is its age consistent across locations?Cloth samples
    AMS radiocarbon · textiles · statistics

    Why it ranks first: the published radiocarbon result is the strongest direct calendar measurement to build on. The 1988 material came from one location, divided among laboratories; a new study should improve spatial coverage. The 2026 examination of retained Arizona fragments found no repair or significant contamination in those fragments. Neither observation makes a fresh result a foregone conclusion. [7] [8] [9]

    The study

    Map the textile first, then select several separated locations confirmed as main-cloth linen. Record yarn continuity, repairs, and deposits. Validate cellulose preparation on known-age controls; distribute coded, sufficiently sized splits among independent AMS laboratories. Archive material and publish locations, masses, preparation yields, blanks, raw results, and calibrated intervals.

    The decision

    Concordant results across locations would strengthen the age estimate. Reproducible differences would direct investigation toward specific yarns, contamination, or interventions. Separate location effects from laboratory and preparation effects using a registered analysis plan.

    The limit

    Radiocarbon dates the carbon incorporated by the flax. It does not date the later formation of an image in that cellulose, identify its subject, or establish who manufactured the cloth. Sampling requires approved, sufficient material; more readings of one corner do not provide wider coverage.

    First deliverable

    A mapped sampling design and a blinded preparation trial, followed by a spatially explicit chronology if sampling proceeds.

  2. 02Identify the material that makes the image.What is colored, where is the color, and which mechanisms can explain it?Mapped fibers
    Microscopy · analytical chemistry · conservation

    Why it matters: Heller and Adler reported image-related changes in cellulose and argued that pigment did not account for the body image. A modern, independently checked material signature would constrain every formation theory, including sunlight. This project concerns the body image; the red stains have their own study below. [3] [2]

    The study

    Compare precisely located image and nearby background fibers, with separate scorch, treatment, adhesive, and mounting controls. Combine microscopy with complementary chemical measurements, including infrared or Raman microspectroscopy and elemental analysis where validated sensitivity makes them useful. Distinguish altered cellulose, surface coatings, particles, and mixtures.

    The decision

    Quantify coloration around and within individual fibers, through threads, and across the weave. These are different scales. Then test coded sunlight, thermal, contact, pigment, and chemical replicas against the same signature. A necessary, reproducible mismatch can exclude a specified recipe.

    The limit

    Establish spatial resolution and detection limits before claiming a layer thickness or an absence of pigment. A chemical change may have several possible causes. Existing adhesive-tape samples may select particular fibers; new microsamples need conservation approval.

    First deliverable

    An interlaboratory benchmark of image and background material, including spectra, microscopy, detection limits, and uncertainty.

  3. 03Build a calibrated atlas of both surfaces.Which visible features survive controlled measurement?On-cloth imaging
    Photometry · spectroscopy · image registration

    Why it matters: different scans, lighting, and contrast settings can produce different apparent features. The historical photography and spectroscopy programs provide a foundation for a shared measurement atlas. This project establishes the image data used by the other studies. [4] [2]

    The study

    Record both sides under measured illumination with radiometric targets, resolution tests, and repeat exposures. Register visible and near-infrared images, justified dose-limited fluorescence, and close views of image, background, folds, stains, and repairs. Preserve raw files and acquisition records alongside every processed layer.

    The decision

    Measure absolute and relative reflectance, edge widths, fine texture, and regional variability. Test whether apparent reverse-side features persist after accounting for transmitted light and the support behind the cloth. Record weave and current cloth shape independently of image intensity.

    The limit

    A 3D scan measures the cloth’s present surface, not a lost body beneath it. Imaging itself need not remove material, but illumination dose and physical access to both surfaces must be planned. A spectacular photograph without calibration is not the same deliverable.

    First deliverable

    A registered, calibrated atlas with repeatability estimates and reusable image, background, stain, and weave masks.

  4. 04Put Shadow Shroud through an independent replication.Can painted glass and sunlight match the optical and material evidence together?Replica lab + Shroud benchmarks
    Optics · photochemistry · independent replication

    Why this project: Wilson’s account reports a painted pane, moving-sun exposures, and a stationary-lamp comparison. His FAQ explicitly leaves microscopic and chemical agreement untested. That is the gap to close. Historical studies provide reflectance, fluorescence, and fiber observations to test against, with their sampling limits retained. [1] [2] [3]

    Our assessment

    Shadow Shroud merits a dedicated controlled replication: it offers a straightforward route to an inverted, softened image. Its credibility as an explanation of the Shroud depends on whether one reproducible process also matches the material evidence. The browser simulator currently assumes bleaching behavior; it does not establish that match.

    A
    Optical transport

    Measure the light.

    First expose simple stripes, edges, and gray steps through measured glass. Vary pane–cloth gap and orientation. Record spectral irradiance at the linen, glass transmission, paint opacity, temperature, humidity, and time.

    Compare moving illumination with stationary illumination at matched total dose and comparable spectra. Include clear glass, an opaque shield, and no-glass controls. Predict the edge spread from geometry before exposing a new pattern.

    Decision: Can measured optics predict contrast and blur on an unseen exposure, within uncertainty?
    B
    Linen photochemistry

    Measure the fibers.

    Use a documented range of unbleached linens and surface preparations, with untreated and dark-stored controls from each batch. Any artificial aging is a separate experimental variable. Measure before and after exposure, in both sheltered and illuminated regions.

    Compare reflectance spectra, fluorescence, chemical markers, distribution of colored fibrils, and color depth through threads and individual fibers. Measure both faces of the fabric. Distinguish bleaching of a coating from alteration of cellulose.

    Decision: Can the same material and exposure match the image–background differences, including where the color resides?
    C
    Independent replication

    Freeze the recipe.

    Choose the recipe using pilot data, then have a second laboratory repeat it with coded specimens and independent analysis. Register endpoints and acceptance ranges before seeing the replication results.

    Include Beauchamp’s design for historical continuity and a painting made without tracing the Shroud. Keep target-derived masks as transfer demonstrations. Compare with contact and thermal reference samples using the same measurements.

    Decision: Does one frozen recipe reproduce the optical and material signature together, across independent batches?

    Replication unit: an independently prepared and exposed swatch, not a pixel or an individual fiber from one swatch. Randomize positions and batch order. Use the pilot’s between-swatch variation to set a confirmatory sample size; report every exposure, including failures.

    What would each outcome mean?
    ObservationInterpretationNext funding decision
    A recognizable negative, with mismatched chemistry or color depthAn image-transfer demonstration. The tested recipe is inconsistent with those Shroud benchmarks.Stop that recipe or justify a specific revision before another trial.
    Good material agreement, but necessary exposure erases the measured detailThe optical and chemical requirements cannot yet be met together.Map the tradeoff across the declared range of gaps, doses, and materials.

    A negative result rejects the specified recipe and parameter range. It does not rule out every imaginable light-based process. A positive result establishes consistency with the tested evidence, not historical authorship.

    First deliverable

    An open pilot dataset: linen provenance, optical measurements, exposure logs, spectra, microscopy, uncertainty estimates, and a preregistered plan for the independent trial.

  5. 05Test the claimed 3D information against known geometry.Does brightness recover shape reliably, or merely produce a recognizable relief?Images + physical controls
    Computer vision · geometry · experimental imaging

    Why it matters: the 1984 work related intensity to modeled cloth–body distance; later contact simulations compare different underlying shapes. Those are stronger, more specific claims than “a face appears when brightness becomes height.” This project tests geometric inference using the atlas from priority 3. [5] [6]

    The study

    Measure known 3D forms and their draped cloths. Image controlled contact transfers, paintings, ordinary portraits, and shadow replicas using a common acquisition pipeline. Compare direct brightness relief with explicit cloth–body models. Set transformations on training examples, then evaluate withheld shapes and regions.

    The decision

    Report depth errors against the known forms and sensitivity to drape, crop, illumination, and tonal processing. For the Shroud and replicas, compare variation at weave and facial scales under matched resolution. Test whether apparent fine spikes persist across repeated images or arise from fabric and photographic processing.

    The limit

    There is no known original face with which to score the Shroud’s anatomical accuracy. A method succeeding on controls supports that method under those conditions. Spikiness alone neither establishes nor rules out a sunlight image; the current simulator does not model individual fibers.

    First deliverable

    An open benchmark with measured geometry, fixed processing, withheld test cases, and reproducible error and texture comparisons.

  6. 06Reconstruct the cloth’s manufacture and repairs.What was woven together, and what was added or altered later?Cloth + textile collections
    Textile archaeology · conservation · materials history

    Why it matters: the 2026 examination connects retained dating fragments to textile structure while acknowledging their limited coverage. A whole-cloth manufacturing and intervention map is a different task from obtaining more radiocarbon readings—and is essential to choosing representative samples. [9]

    The study

    Map weave, warp and weft density, yarn twist, joins, seams, selvages, irregularities, patches, and damage. Trace yarn continuity where observable. Compare measurements with securely dated textiles using an explicit comparison set, and reconcile physical interventions with conservation records.

    The decision

    Identify which regions belong to the same textile and where discontinuities support a repair or addition. Test proposed manufacturing traditions against measured combinations of features, including counterexamples. Feed that map into the dating, imaging, and trace-sampling plans.

    The limit

    A weave type alone does not provide a calendar date or a unique place of manufacture. Textile comparisons depend on what has survived and been excavated. Unusual features must be assessed against the gaps in the comparative record.

    First deliverable

    A yarn, seam, repair, and conservation map with a documented comparative textile catalogue and confidence assigned to each interpretation.

  7. 07Determine what the red stains contain and how they overlap.Which biological or mineral substances are present, and can any sequence of events be resolved?Mapped stain samples
    Forensic chemistry · proteomics · microscopy

    Why it matters: the historical chemical work interpreted stain components as blood, using analyses distinct from those of the body image. Independent, spatially controlled testing would clarify composition and test claims about the order in which stains and image formed. [3]

    The study

    Compare several mapped red-stain regions with nearby unstained fibers, conservation materials, known biological stains, and pigment/binder controls. Combine molecular and protein evidence with spectroscopy and particle microscopy. Examine well-preserved overlap boundaries; test proposed layering interpretations on replicas made in known sequences.

    The decision

    Require converging, specific evidence for candidate blood components, pigments, binders, or mixtures. Where resolution and preservation permit, determine which material lies above or below another. Report differences between stain regions rather than assigning one explanation to every red mark.

    The limit

    Iron or protein alone is not a blood identification. Even confirmed human blood would not identify its donor or prove a particular injury or event. Failure to resolve an image beneath a stain is not, by itself, a reliable chronology.

    First deliverable

    A blinded stain-composition study and a map of overlap regions that can—or cannot—support conclusions about relative sequence.

  8. 08Audit the historical chain of custody.Which records and objects can be securely connected to this particular cloth?Archives + historical objects
    History · manuscript studies · art history

    Why it matters: laboratory dates cannot supply missing ownership records. Documentary editions and museum-catalogued objects provide evidence that can be checked independently of an image-formation theory. They also require source criticism rather than uncritical repetition of later narratives. [12]

    The study

    Build a linked dossier of original documents, surviving copies, translations, inventories, custody records, and depictions. Record the physical document’s date separately from the events it describes. Recheck transcriptions against originals where available; compare object-specific dimensions, paired images, damage, and heraldry with securely dated material.

    The decision

    Identify the earliest secure attestations, supported transfers, and unresolved gaps. Define in advance what evidence would connect a proposed earlier cloth to this one. Compare competing interpretations with the same source set, and distinguish absence of a record from evidence that an event did not happen.

    The limit

    A reference to a burial cloth or a similar-looking face is not automatically a reference to the Turin Shroud. A later copy may preserve an older report but needs its own provenance. A secure historical appearance does not necessarily date manufacture.

    First deliverable

    An openly documented chronology with images or repository references, transcriptions, translations, disputed readings, and confidence for each proposed connection.

  9. 09Identify pollen and particles in mapped context.What exposure or treatment history can the traces actually distinguish?Mapped particles + controls
    Palynology · mineralogy · archaeological residues

    Why it matters: Boi’s reassessment of published images proposed different pollen identifications from earlier accounts. Fresh mapped samples and independent identification could address that uncertainty. More grains alone would not resolve when or how they arrived. [11]

    The study

    Collect localized particles before other work disturbs them. Keep front, reverse, edges, folds, and backing samples separate. Include collection blanks, ambient dust, and storage-material controls. Independent palynologists should identify coded grains against reference collections; publish images, counts, taxonomic confidence, and disagreements alongside associated minerals and residues.

    The decision

    Test whether reproducible assemblages differ from contemporary contamination and fit particular exposure or treatment histories better than alternatives. Consider later flowers, handling, storage, trade, and transported materials. Where identifications stop at genus or family, preserve that uncertainty in any geographic inference.

    The limit

    A grain’s plant identity does not tell when it reached the cloth. Species distributions alone cannot establish first-century Jerusalem. Dating a tiny, mixed pollen sample should not be promised without feasibility work and a securely attributable fraction. We rank this below direct material and chronology studies.

    First deliverable

    A reproducible particle atlas and a spatially controlled assessment of which historical interpretations remain distinguishable.

  10. 10Authenticate DNA before interpreting its origin.Can any older signal be separated from documented handling and laboratory contamination?Sequence data + mapped samples
    Ancient DNA · metagenomics · contamination analysis

    Why it matters: the July 2026 study reported multiple biological sources and a dominant human lineage matching the historical sample collector. Direct access would improve sample provenance but would not remove earlier contacts. [10]

    The study

    Start with independent reanalysis of available sequences and collection records. If new sampling is informative, collect mapped regions and blanks before DNA-altering treatments, with replicate extraction in separate laboratories. Estimate mixtures, damage, fragment length, mapping uncertainty, and coverage; use appropriately consented handler comparisons where helpful.

    The decision

    Ask whether a candidate signal recurs in independently documented samples, survives contamination models, and has evidence supporting an older origin. Assess microbial and other environmental signals as possible contact or preservation evidence. Let those results determine whether more sampling would add information.

    The limit

    Damage is not a precise calendar clock, a geographic lineage is not a travel itinerary, and a biological trace is not automatically from the figure represented. Without an attributable reference, DNA cannot identify Jesus. Identifiable contemporary donor information requires protection.

    First deliverable

    A contamination audit, a reproducible assessment of candidate older signals, and a clear recommendation on whether further sampling is justified.

The same standard across all ten

A result someone
else can check.

Register the hypothesis, parameter range, comparison set, and decision rules before confirmatory work. Measure uncertainty and independent replication. Preserve chain of custody, raw observations, calibration, analysis code, and negative results.

AI can help register images, compare models, and organize source evidence when checked against known controls. Generated detail remains a hypothesis or illustration. A convincing face is not a new measurement.

Together these studies could substantially clarify the cloth’s age, material, formation constraints, and documented history. They may leave more than one explanation viable. No combination of attractive images, old fibers, or biological traces automatically identifies the person represented.
