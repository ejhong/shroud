A research agenda / Ten priorities

Ten paths to clarity.

The most useful next questions about the cloth’s age, image, and history.

Ranked by likely information gained, usefulness across hypotheses, and feasibility. This is our assessment of selected literature through September 2026, not a consensus ranking.

Access and study order

Priority is not visit order. Document before sampling, collect vulnerable traces before disruptive work, and coordinate light exposure with biological analysis. Cloth access and sampling need custodial approval; replica and archive work can proceed independently.

The ten studies

What would each answer?

Open a study for its method and limits.

  1. 01Date linen from several locations.How old is the cloth across its main body?Cloth samples
    AMS radiocarbon · textiles · statistics

    The radiocarbon result is the strongest direct calendar measurement. Its samples came from one location; a 2026 examination found no repair in retained Arizona fragments. [7] [8] [9]

    The study

    Map the textile, then sample several separated main-cloth locations. Compare coded splits across independent AMS labs, with validated cellulose preparation and known-age controls. Publish locations, yields, blanks, and raw results.

    The decision

    Agreement would strengthen the age estimate. Reproducible differences would guide investigation of particular yarns, contamination, or repairs. Separate location, laboratory, and preparation effects.

    The limit

    Radiocarbon dates flax carbon, not the image or its subject. More measurements of one corner do not improve spatial coverage.

    First deliverable

    A registered sampling plan, independent dates, and an openly documented material archive.

  2. 02Identify the image’s chemistry.What is colored, and where does that color reside?Mapped fibers
    Microscopy · analytical chemistry · conservation

    Heller and Adler reported cellulose changes and argued that pigment did not explain the body image. Independent material benchmarks would constrain every formation theory. [3] [2]

    The study

    Compare mapped image and background fibers with scorch, treatment, and adhesive controls. Combine microscopy, validated infrared or Raman measurements, and elemental analysis to distinguish cellulose changes, coatings, and particles.

    The decision

    Measure color within fibers, through threads, and across the weave. Compare coded sunlight, thermal, contact, pigment, and chemical replicas against the same signature.

    The limit

    Resolution and detection limits bound claims about color depth or absent pigment. Tape samples can select particular fibers; one chemical change may have several causes.

    First deliverable

    An interlaboratory image–background benchmark with spectra, microscopy, and uncertainty.

  3. 03Map both sides of the cloth.Which features survive calibrated measurement?On-cloth imaging
    Photometry · spectroscopy · image registration

    Lighting, scanning, and contrast change apparent features. Historical photography and spectroscopy provide a starting point for a shared measurement atlas. [4] [2]

    The study

    Record both sides under measured light with reference targets and repeat exposures. Register visible, near-infrared, justified dose-limited fluorescence, and close views. Preserve raw files and acquisition records.

    The decision

    Measure reflectance, edges, texture, and regional variation. Separate possible reverse-side features from transmitted light and backing effects. Record weave and current cloth shape independently.

    The limit

    A 3D scan measures today’s cloth surface, not a lost body. Light dose and access to both sides require conservation planning.

    First deliverable

    A calibrated atlas with repeatability estimates and reusable image, stain, and weave masks.

  4. 04Replicate Shadow Shroud.Can one recipe match the optics and material evidence?Replica lab + Shroud benchmarks
    Optics · photochemistry · independent replication

    Wilson reported sunlight and lamp exposures but left microscopic and chemical agreement untested. Replication should compare both optics and material properties against measured Shroud benchmarks. [1] [2] [3]

    Our assessment

    The mechanism can invert and soften an image. The decisive question is whether the same process matches the fibers. Our simulator assumes bleaching; it cannot establish that material match.

    A
    Optical transport

    Measure the light.

    Expose stripes, edges, and gray steps through measured glass. Vary gap and orientation; record spectral irradiance, glass transmission, paint opacity, temperature, humidity, and time.

    Compare moving and fixed light at equal dose and comparable spectra. Include clear-glass, opaque-shield, and no-glass controls. Predict a new pattern’s edge spread before exposure.

    Decision: Do predictions match contrast and blur in an unseen exposure?
    B
    Linen photochemistry

    Measure the fibers.

    Test documented linens and surface preparations against untreated and dark-stored controls. Measure sheltered and lit regions before and after exposure. Treat artificial aging as a separate variable.

    Measure spectra, fluorescence, chemistry, colored fibrils, and color depth within fibers and threads, on both sides. Distinguish a bleached coating from altered cellulose.

    Decision: Does one recipe match image–background differences and color depth?
    C
    Independent replication

    Freeze the recipe.

    Select a pilot recipe, then register endpoints and acceptance ranges. A second lab repeats it with coded specimens and independent analysis.

    Use Beauchamp’s design and an independent painting without Shroud pixels. Include contact and thermal controls. Target-derived masks remain transfer demonstrations.

    Decision: Does the fixed recipe replicate both signatures across batches?

    Replicate swatches, not pixels. Randomize positions and batches. Use between-swatch variation to size the confirmatory trial; report every exposure and failure.

    First deliverable

    An open pilot dataset and a preregistered independent trial.

  5. 05Test the claimed 3D information.Does brightness recover geometry reliably?Images + physical controls
    Computer vision · geometry · experimental imaging

    Intensity–distance research and contact simulations make testable geometric claims. A recognizable brightness relief alone is a weaker observation. [5] [6]

    The study

    Use measured 3D forms and draped cloths. Photograph contact transfers, paintings, portraits, and shadow replicas consistently. Set processing on training examples, then test withheld shapes and regions.

    The decision

    Score depth errors against known forms. Compare Shroud and replica texture at matched resolution. Check whether fine spikes persist across images or arise from weave and processing.

    The limit

    No original face is available to score Shroud anatomy. Spikiness alone neither confirms nor excludes sunlight; this simulator does not model fibers. Explore lateral distortion in the cloth lab ↗

    First deliverable

    Known geometry, fixed processing, withheld examples, and reproducible error comparisons.

  6. 06Trace manufacture and repairs.What was woven together or added later?Cloth + textile collections
    Textile archaeology · conservation · materials history

    Retained dating fragments offer a limited textile sample. A whole-cloth map would test manufacturing history and guide representative sampling. [9]

    The study

    Map weave, yarn density and twist, seams, selvages, repairs, and damage. Trace yarn continuity; compare securely dated textiles and conservation records.

    The decision

    Identify continuous cloth and supported additions. Test manufacturing traditions against combinations of features and counterexamples. Use the findings to guide dating and trace sampling.

    The limit

    Weave alone gives neither a calendar date nor a unique origin. Comparative collections reflect uneven survival and excavation.

    First deliverable

    A textile and repair map with a comparative catalogue and confidence for each interpretation.

  7. 07Analyze the red stains.What do they contain, and which layer came first?Mapped stain samples
    Forensic chemistry · proteomics · microscopy

    Historical chemical work interpreted stain components as blood. Composition and overlap need independent tests separate from those of the body image. [3]

    The study

    Compare mapped stains, nearby fibers, conservation materials, biological stains, and pigment/binder controls. Combine molecular evidence, proteomics, spectroscopy, and microscopy. Test layering interpretations on known-sequence replicas.

    The decision

    Require specific, converging evidence for blood components, pigments, or mixtures. Resolve material order only where preservation and resolution permit; compare distinct stain regions.

    The limit

    Iron or protein alone does not identify blood. Human blood would not identify a donor or event. An unresolved image beneath a stain does not establish chronology.

    First deliverable

    A blinded composition study and an overlap map showing where sequence can be resolved.

  8. 08Audit the historical record.Which records securely identify this cloth?Archives + historical objects
    History · manuscript studies · art history

    Laboratory dates cannot fill gaps in ownership. Manuscripts, depictions, and museum objects offer independently checkable evidence. [12]

    The study

    Link documents, surviving copies, translations, inventories, and depictions. Separate each document’s date from its reported events. Verify transcriptions and compare dimensions, paired images, damage, and heraldry.

    The decision

    Establish secure attestations, transfers, and gaps. Define what would connect an earlier cloth to this one; test competing readings against the same sources.

    The limit

    A burial-cloth reference or similar face need not describe the Turin Shroud. A secure appearance does not date manufacture; absent records do not prove absent events.

    First deliverable

    A documented chronology with repository references, translations, disputes, and confidence levels.

  9. 09Identify pollen and particles.Which exposure histories can the traces distinguish?Mapped particles + controls
    Palynology · mineralogy · archaeological residues

    Boi proposed revised pollen identifications from published images. Fresh mapped samples and independent identification could address that uncertainty. [11]

    The study

    Collect localized particles before disturbance. Separate front, reverse, folds, edges, and backing, with ambient and collection blanks. Have independent specialists identify coded grains against reference collections; publish images, counts, and uncertainty.

    The decision

    Test whether reproducible assemblages differ from contemporary contamination and favor particular histories. Include flowers, handling, storage, trade, and transported materials as alternatives.

    The limit

    Plant identity does not date arrival. Species alone cannot establish first-century Jerusalem. Dating tiny or mixed pollen fractions requires feasibility work.

    First deliverable

    A particle atlas and a controlled comparison of distinguishable exposure histories.

  10. 10Authenticate the DNA traces.Can older signals be separated from handling?Sequence data + mapped samples
    Ancient DNA · metagenomics · contamination analysis

    The 2026 study found mixed sources and a dominant human lineage matching the historical collector. New access would not remove earlier contamination. [10]

    The study

    Reanalyze available sequences first. If justified, collect mapped samples and blanks before DNA-altering treatments. Replicate in independent labs; assess mixtures, damage, fragment length, mapping, and consented handler comparisons.

    The decision

    Check whether signals recur independently and survive contamination models. Assess environmental DNA as contact or preservation evidence; let the results guide further sampling.

    The limit

    Damage is not a calendar clock, lineage is not an itinerary, and DNA cannot identify Jesus without an attributable reference. Protect identifiable modern donor data.

    First deliverable

    A contamination audit and evidence-based recommendation on further sampling.

A shared standard

Make every result checkable.

Register hypotheses, comparisons, and decision rules. Report uncertainty and independent replication. Preserve custody records, calibration, raw data, code, and negative results.

AI can align images and compare models against controls. Generated detail is not a measurement.

These studies could clarify age, materials, and history while leaving several explanations viable. They cannot automatically identify the figure.
