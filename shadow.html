The reproduction challenge

Work backward from the face.

Given the photograph, can this model find a painting that reproduces its tones? Choose a Shroud-derived mask, then let the search adjust the paint under your current conditions. It is an inverse-design exercise: the target supplies the desired image.

Start with a Shroud-derived face or full-cloth mask.

The search uses 24 constrained steps, keeping paint values between clear and opaque. It does not recreate a medieval painting process. The resulting mask is loaded into the experiment above; you can change its exposure or examine it in 3D.

An experiment within the experiment

Does the result survive a change?

Compare the current painting at three gaps, under moving and stationary light. All six use the same total exposure. Each result is computed from the current settings; select one to load it above.

Three ways into the question

Try changing just one thing.

Hold the sun still.

Switch to the stationary control. It receives the same integrated exposure as the moving sun, so differences come from shadow geometry, including the angular size of the sun.

Lift the painting.

Try 0, 12, and 35 mm of air. Top-painted glass still creates separation even at zero air gap. Paint on the underside of glass at zero gap to remove it.

Attempt a reproduction.

Choose the Shroud face or full-cloth mask. A resemblance is then partly supplied by the input image. This tests image transfer, while the independent brush study tests the mechanism without those pixels.
